# Production Expansion Lessons

This cumulative register records reusable controls without forcing countries into one hierarchy.

## Accepted pilots carried into production

- Tunisia: crossing boundaries require explicit non-parent relationships; denominator scope is layer/date-specific.
- Jordan: legal amendments must be applied temporally and municipalities may be parallel to the legal hierarchy.
- Saudi Arabia: occurrence-heavy sources require parent-scoped identity and explicit exclusions; national denominator conflicts remain visible.
- United Arab Emirates: country-internal profiles need contextual types and authorities rather than one artificial lower tier.

## Bahrain production cycle

- Official bilingual time-series records can close a current first layer only after filtering one explicit year.
- Retrieval date, data year, and legal/historical transition are separate facts.
- A historical fifth governorate must not remain current; it also must not be reconstructed without an atomic historical topology source.
- Serial heritage property identity is distinct from component parts and local administrative assignment.
- Official open-data reuse policy is an atomic source, not an assumption.
- Complete World Heritage coverage does not imply broad cultural or place coverage.
- Unsupported dialect and lower-area layers should remain documented absences, not generated templates.

## Controls now required for subsequent countries

1. Exact current-layer denominator with source, date, license, and exclusion definition.
2. Mutation for historical-as-current where a reorganization exists.
3. Mutation preventing address/statistical units from becoming administrative entities.
4. Country-specific cultural-leakage and source-country tests.
5. Full review where a new production country's bounded record universe is small.
6. Separate statement of unavailable populated-place, neighborhood, cultural, and dialect denominators.
