Culture of the United Arab Emirates (English mirror). The UAE's official language is Arabic, but English is widely spoken due to the country's diverse nature and economic globalization. Farsi, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali and Mandarin are also widely spoken by expatriates. Native Emirati nationals speak the Gulf Arabic, similar to that spoken in other GCC countries and Iraq. Naming conventions: the first name is followed by 'bin' or 'bint'; after marriage, women retain their family names, and children take the name of the father. A dallah is a traditional Arabic coffee pot for serving Arabian coffee; it is a symbol of the Emirati culture, featuring on the dirham coin. Al Ain in the Emirate of Abu Dhabi is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Sharjah was named 'The Cultural capital of the Arab World' by UNESCO in 1998 and 'Capital of Islamic Culture for 2014' by the OIC. Emirati culture is a blend of Arabian, Islamic and Persian cultures, with influences from East Africa and the Indian subcontinent; Islam has had a prominent influence on local architecture, music, attire, cuisine and lifestyle.
